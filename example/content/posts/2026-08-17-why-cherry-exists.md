Cherry 0.1.0 went stable this week, so this feels like the right moment to write down why it exists at all. The short version: I missed Octopress, and I could not forgive what happened to every Octopress blog ever customized.

If you were around in 2011, you remember the feeling. A blog that was *yours*, hackable to the bone, built by tools you understood. And then you touched one theme file, and from that moment your blog was frozen in time: every upstream fix, every improvement, walled off behind a fork you had silently committed to maintaining forever. Most of those blogs died of that. Mine did.

Cherry is my answer to the question: what would that tool look like if we built it now, knowing what we know?

## Four convictions

**A build is a function.** Same tree in, same bytes out, every time, on every machine. Cherry's CI double-builds its fixture sites on every commit and fails if one byte differs. That sounds like discipline for its own sake until you feel the consequences: deploy diffs that mean something, caches that actually work, and the quiet confidence that when the output changed, *you* changed it. There are no timestamps in the output, no "generated by" footers, no map iteration order leaking into HTML. Boring by design, and boring is the feature.

**The verifier is the product.** Most static site generators render whatever you feed them and let production find the broken link. Cherry's `check` builds your entire site in memory, writes nothing, and reports problems as structured diagnostics, each one naming the file that would fix it:

```text
[error] content/pages/reading.md: broken-link — links to /guides/pruning/, which this build does not emit
```

The rules go deeper than links: schema-validated frontmatter (an unknown key is an error, not a mystery), images without alt text, duplicate titles, scaffold fields nobody filled in, feed sanity. The generated deploy workflow runs `check --strict` before it builds, so the class of mistake that used to live on production simply cannot arrive there.

**One binary.** Cherry ships as a single self-contained executable with the whole BEAM inside, thanks to [Burrito](https://github.com/burrito-elixir/burrito). `curl | sh`, and you have the entire toolchain: builder, verifier, dev server, theme system, search indexer. `cherry upgrade` swaps the binary in place, checksum-verified against the release. Elixir folk can take the hex package instead and get every verb as a mix task; the two lanes build byte-identical sites, by construction and by test.

**Agents are users.** Every verb takes `--json` and returns one envelope shape with honest exit codes. `cherry schema posts` answers "what does a valid file look like" before an agent writes a single line. Every build emits `llms.txt` and a markdown mirror of every page, so the published site is legible without a DOM parser. The scaffold writes an `AGENTS.md`. None of this was bolted on at the end; the CLI was designed against the question "could a machine drive this loop unattended, and would a human enjoy the same interface?" It turns out the answer to both is yes, and it is the same interface.

## And the theming grudge

The Octopress failure mode got a system of its own. Themes expose **tokens** as their public styling API. Changing your accent color is one config line, not a fork. When you do need to own a template, `theme.eject` records provenance (theme, version, content hash), and `theme.diff` gives you a three-way answer after every upgrade: `current`, `auto_updatable`, or `conflict`, with `--apply` for the safe case. Your customization and the upstream's progress stop being enemies.

That is 0.1.0. A blog engine, a portfolio, a CV, feeds, search, SEO, deploys, all of it deterministic and all of it checked. The [quick-start](/guides/quick-start/) is fifteen minutes if you want the feeling rather than the philosophy.

What is next is already brewing: we have been rethinking what styling and templates should feel like for someone who lives in modern CSS and Phoenix. More on that in [the next post](/what-building-0-2-0-taught-us/).
